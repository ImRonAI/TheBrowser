# Strengths: LGBTQIA+ people in the Coachella Valley

Review date: 2026-11-08.

This dossier records strengths that retrieved sources locate in the Coachella Valley, Riverside County, California, or in a named city. Palm Springs is not treated as the valley. A strength is labeled Palm Springs, Cathedral City, Rancho Mirage, Desert Hot Springs, Palm Desert, La Quinta, Indio, Coachella, or another stated geography when the source is that specific. Where a source does not split west valley from east valley, no split is inferred. Sexual orientation, sexual behavior, and gender identity are kept distinct. Same-sex couple counts are household structure, not an LGBT population count. Social-media posts are not used as prevalence. No private individual is identified.

A strength is included only when an opened page or a fetched PubMed record supports both the fact and a mechanism for why it exists here or how it could affect health. Organizational self-description is labeled as such.

## How to read geography

HARC defines the Coachella Valley, for the 2022 survey, as nine cities (Cathedral City, Coachella, Desert Hot Springs, Indian Wells, Indio, La Quinta, Palm Desert, Palm Springs, and Rancho Mirage) and 12 census-designated places (Bermuda Dunes, Desert Edge, Desert Palms, Indio Hills, Garnet, Mecca, North Shore, Oasis, Sky Valley, Thermal, Thousand Palms, and Vista Santa Rosa), in eastern Riverside County (HARC, Inc., 2023). That definition is HARC's survey geography. It is not a license to assign a Palm Springs finding to Indio, Mecca, or Thermal.

This dossier does not apply a west-valley or east-valley label unless the source uses one. The Center's own pages call Coachella, and the unincorporated communities of Mecca, Thermal, and North Shore, the East Valley or Eastern Coachella Valley. Those labels are reported as the organization's labels.

## Strength 1. A measured concentration of LGBQ identification, including among adults 55 and older

**Statement.** In the 2022 Coachella Valley Community Health Survey, 21.0% of adults were reported as identifying their sexual orientation as lesbian, gay, bisexual, questioning, or other. The published categories were heterosexual 79.0% (population estimate 232,632), homosexual 16.3% (48,045), bisexual 2.6% (7,578), and questioning or other 2.1% (6,187), on a sexual-orientation denominator of 294,442. HARC states that this LGBQ share is significantly larger than in Riverside County or California, and that the percent identifying as gay, lesbian, or homosexual is four times the California percent. Among adults HARC defines as seniors (55 and older), the report's prose says nearly 22.0% identify as LGBQ. The senior table prints homosexual 20.2% (31,228), bisexual 2.1% (3,284), and questioning or another sexual orientation 1.7% (2,642). Those three count cells are arithmetically consistent with their printed percents against the printed total of 154,637. The printed heterosexual percent (78.0%) is not consistent with its printed count (117,483 of 154,637), and the prose "nearly 22.0%" is not consistent with the sum of the three non-heterosexual counts. Those two conflicting figures are not used as estimates.

**Geography.** Coachella Valley as HARC defined it in 2022. The sexual-orientation section retrieved does not disaggregate by city. No east-valley rate is inferred.

**Population and identity measurement.** Sexual orientation, not gender identity and not sexual behavior. HARC's prose says adults "identify their sexual orientation" and defines sexual orientation as attraction and desire for relationships. The exact survey stem was not in the extracted lines. Table labels use "homosexual," which collapses gay and lesbian and is not a gender-identity measure. Gender was measured separately with the Williams Institute two-question method (sex assigned at birth, then current gender identity). Current identity was male 52.8% (176,879), female 46.8% (156,707), transgender 0.3% (1,061), and do not identify as female, male, or transgender 0.1% (256). Separately, 0.9% (3,130) had a sex assigned at birth that did not match current gender identity. Those gender figures are not an LGBT count and are not added to the sexual-orientation figures.

**Sourced mechanism.** HARC states that the valley has long been a welcoming place for lesbian, gay, bisexual, queer, and questioning populations, and it points to Census 2010 same-sex-couple rankings as the historical marker of that concentration (see Strength 2 for the primary ranking table). HARC separately states that adults in the valley are significantly older than adults in Riverside County and California because the valley is a major retirement destination, and that the senior LGBQ share is even higher than the all-adult share, "indicating that our Valley is home to many LGBQ seniors." The health pathway HARC itself states is informational rather than clinical: before HARC, organizations relied on county data that did not describe valley-specific needs, and the triennial survey exists so local leaders can turn valley data into action. HARC does not claim that a higher LGBQ percentage is itself a health outcome.

**Limits.** Address-based sample of private residences, April–August 2022; 2,790 completed surveys (2,447 adult; 343 child); 8.4% completed in Spanish. People in group homes, nursing homes, assisted living, jails, prisons, and people who were homeless were excluded. Estimates are weighted percents and population estimates; the weighting source sentence was not extracted. Sexual-orientation and sex-at-birth denominators differ (294,442 versus 335,848), so item nonresponse is likely. The California comparison is HARC's statement about CHIS 2021; the chart's city-or-state point estimates were not cleanly extracted, so no CHIS percentage is repeated here. HARC's statement that Palm Springs ranked number 1 nationally is not used; the opened Williams Institute snapshot ranks Palm Springs number 1 in California and number 3 in the United States among cities with 50 or more same-sex couples (Strength 2). No city-level sexual-orientation table was retrieved, so this strength is not evidence that Indio, Coachella, Mecca, or Thermal has the valley-wide percentage.

**Citation.** HARC, Inc. (2023). *2022 Coachella Valley Community Health Survey*. https://kesq.b-cdn.net/2023/03/HARC-2022-Executive-Report.pdf

## Strength 2. High same-sex-couple household density in several named cities, not an LGBT census

**Statement.** Gates and Cooke, using adjusted Census 2010 counts of cohabiting same-sex couples in cities with 50 or more such couples, rank Palm Springs first in California and third in the United States: 2,440 couples, 107.28 per 1,000 households. In the same city table: Rancho Mirage, California rank 4 and U.S. rank 8, 462 couples, 52.29 per 1,000; Cathedral City, California rank 5 and U.S. rank 11, 790 couples, 46.33 per 1,000; Desert Hot Springs, California rank 10 and U.S. rank 42, 195 couples, 22.56 per 1,000; Palm Desert, California rank 20 and U.S. rank 86, 334 couples, 14.47 per 1,000; La Quinta, California rank 43 and U.S. rank 219, 142 couples, 9.56 per 1,000; Indio, California rank 86 and U.S. rank 465, 165 couples, 7.05 per 1,000. California as a whole had 98,153 same-sex couples and 7.8 per 1,000 households. In that same city table, San Francisco had 10,461 couples and 30.25 per 1,000 (California rank 8, U.S. rank 28); the City of San Diego had 5,910 and 12.23 per 1,000 (California rank 28, U.S. rank 119); the City of Los Angeles had 13,292 and 10.08 per 1,000 (California rank 40, U.S. rank 192). Earlier rows in the PDF with different couple counts are not used, because they sit outside this city-rate table.

**Geography.** Named cities only. The snapshot does not classify cities as west or east valley. Indian Wells, the City of Coachella, and the census-designated places were not in the pattern-matched lines of the cities-with-50-or-more table. Absence from those matches is not an estimate of zero.

**Population and identity measurement.** Household structure. A same-sex couple is a household in which the householder describes another adult as a husband, wife, or unmarried partner of the same sex. The Census did not ask sexual orientation or gender identity. Unpartnered lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, nonbinary, and queer people are not counted. Adjusted estimates attempt to correct sex miscoding. This is not an LGBT prevalence.

**Sourced mechanism.** The snapshot documents where cohabiting same-sex couples were concentrated in 2010. It does not explain the migration history. HARC uses this ranking as evidence that the valley has long been a place with a high same-sex-couple presence (Strength 1). The LGBTQ Community Center of the Desert states that it began in 1998 as the Prime Timers of the Desert Foundation, a social club of mature gay and bisexual men in Palm Springs, later a senior center in Cathedral City, then a SAGE-affiliated center, then a valley LGBTQ center (Strength 5). DAP Health states that it was founded in Palm Springs in 1984 by community volunteers in response to the HIV epidemic in the Coachella Valley (Strength 3). Those institutional histories are the sourced pathways from a concentrated partnered population to health-relevant organizations. The census snapshot itself does not measure a health effect.

**Limits.** Data year is 2010, not 2020. The 2020 Williams Institute city interactive and the press release's small-city ranking were not opened for Palm Springs; the press-release page was not opened. The HARC-cited Williams PDF URL returned 404; the eScholarship copy of Gates and Cooke (2011) was opened instead. Rates are not comparable to HARC's 2022 sexual-orientation percentages. Comparing Palm Springs at 107.28 per 1,000 households with San Francisco at 30.25 uses the same indicator, year, and couple definition; it still does not compare LGBT population shares.

**Citation.** Gates, G. J., & Cooke, A. M. (2011). *California census snapshot: 2010*. Williams Institute, UCLA School of Law. https://escholarship.org/content/qt00t9v0kz/qt00t9v0kz.pdf

## Strength 3. Specialized clinical capacity that began as a local HIV response and now includes prevention, status-neutral care, and named sites outside Palm Springs

**Statement.** DAP Health states that Desert AIDS Project was founded in Palm Springs in 1984 by community volunteers in response to the HIV epidemic in the Coachella Valley; that it opened a satellite office in Indio in 1994 to serve underserved communities; that it opened what it calls the first adult HIV-specialty dental clinic in Riverside County in 2008; that it launched Get Tested Coachella Valley in 2014 and a walk-in HIV/STI clinic, The DOCK, in 2015; that it became DAP Health in 2021; and that it acquired Borrego Health in 2023. It describes itself as a deemed Federally Qualified Health Center receiving HHS funding. Current pages list HIV specialty care at the Sunrise campus in Palm Springs and at Stonewall Medical Center in Cathedral City, and free or low-cost PrEP, PEP, and DoxyPEP, including same-day prescriptions and starter doses. HIV care is offered regardless of insurance status, with sliding-scale payment. The status-neutral description says reactive tests are started immediately on antiretroviral therapy and nonreactive patients are educated on prevention medication. ART is described as lowering virus to undetectable levels and preventing transmission to others when taken consistently (U=U). The Indio Sexual Wellness Clinic, 81719 Doctor Carreon Boulevard, states that it supports Indio, Coachella, and surrounding areas with free STI testing and treatment, PrEP, PEP, pregnancy testing, and birth control, and that it speaks Spanish. Stonewall Medical Center, 68555 Ramon Road, Cathedral City, states that it serves the LGBTQ+ community and the broader Coachella Valley patient population, offers HIV, hepatitis, STI, PrEP, PEP, and mental health services, and speaks Spanish. CDC NPIN lists the Palm Springs site as an FQHC with free HIV testing, PrEP for uninsured people, PEP, HIV medical treatment, ADAP drug purchasing assistance, HOPWA, and PrEP navigation, and lists Get Tested Coachella Valley as a program. NPIN's page header says "Sexual Wellness Services Orange Clinic" while the address is 1695 N Sunrise Way, Palm Springs; the header is not used as a location fact.

**Geography.** Founding and Sunrise campus: Palm Springs. Stonewall and Centro Medico dentistry addresses in the Ryan White list: Cathedral City. Indio sexual wellness: Indio, with the page naming Coachella and surrounding areas as the area supported. The about page also says the current network runs from the Coachella Valley to the San Diego coast, so valley pages must not be read as the whole organization. No Mecca, Thermal, or North Shore clinic address was retrieved.

**Population and identity measurement.** Services are not restricted to one identity. DAP states PrEP and PEP are for anyone who wants protection from HIV, regardless of gender, orientation, or relationship style. NPIN lists audiences that include LGBTQ people and, separately, men who have sex with men. MSM is a behavioral category, not a sexual-orientation identity and not a gender identity. Gender health and wellness is named on the LGBTQ care page; a hormone protocol, eligibility rule, or patient volume for gender-affirming care was not retrieved from an opened DAP page. A provider biography was not used as a volume estimate.

**Sourced mechanism.** DAP states the clinic system exists because local volunteers created it during the local epidemic, then extended it to Indio and later integrated it with Borrego clinics. The health pathway DAP states is direct: PrEP and PEP prevent HIV; immediate ART is described as lifesaving and as making transmission impossible when virus is suppressed; on-site pharmacy, case management, and assistance with housing, food, and transportation are described as keeping people in care; culturally competent care is described as leading to better health outcomes; cost is described as not a barrier because of free programs, sliding scale, and a policy of not turning people away for inability to pay. Those are the organization's claims about mechanism, not an independent outcome evaluation retrieved in this run.

**Limits.** No patient-volume, viral-suppression, or PrEP-coverage statistic was on the opened DAP clinical pages. The phrase "country's largest PrEP intervention," seen only in an unopened press-release snippet, is not used. Harm-reduction counts are in Strength 4. Ryan White funding categories are in Strength 4. ZIP and suite notation for the Indio site differ between the DAP page (92211, Suite 1D) and the county Ryan White list (92201, Suite D1); both are reported as printed, not reconciled.

## Strength 4. Ryan White and county HIV services reach Palm Springs, Cathedral City, and Indio, with much thinner county hours in Indio

**Statement.** San Bernardino County Department of Public Health, acting as the listing agency for Ryan White Part A, Part B, and Ending the HIV Epidemic services in the Riverside/San Bernardino area, lists DAP Health as a funded provider in Palm Springs (case management, emergency financial assistance, early intervention, food, housing, medical case management, medical nutrition therapy, mental health, outpatient care, psychosocial support, outpatient substance-use services, and transportation), Cathedral City (case management, early intervention, medical case management, and outpatient care, plus a separate general-dentistry oral-health site), and Indio (early intervention and outpatient care). The same page lists Riverside University Health System–Public Health in Indio at 47923 Oasis Street for case management, early intervention, food, medical case management, medical nutrition therapy, and outpatient care, with hours printed as the second Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. RUHS's own prevention page lists PrEP at Indio Family Health Center, 47-923 Oasis Street, as once a month, 8:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., and states that most California residents qualify for free PrEP and that prescribed PrEP is highly effective against sexual and injection-related acquisition. The two Indio schedules were not reconciled.

**Geography.** Palm Springs, Cathedral City, and Indio for DAP Ryan White sites. Indio for the county clinic. Riverside and Perris county sites are outside the valley and are not treated as valley capacity. The grant area is two counties, so a funded provider is not evidence that every valley community has a clinic.

**Population and identity measurement.** Ryan White services on this page are for the funded HIV care continuum, not an LGBT identity program. Identity measurement is not stated on the providers page. PrEP eligibility on the RUHS page is residential and clinical, not an identity test.

**Sourced mechanism.** The county page's mechanism is financing: these agencies are funded to provide the listed medical and support services. DAP's mechanism for what that care does is in Strength 3 (ART, U=U, support services). RUHS's mechanism for PrEP is pharmaceutical prevention of HIV acquisition when taken as prescribed, with a free-access claim for most California residents. The health effect is therefore access to funded HIV care and prevention, not a retrieved suppression or incidence rate.

**Limits.** Dollar amounts were not on the opened providers page. A board agenda stating a DAP contract amount was not opened and is not used. Indio county hours are a limit, not a claim of equivalent east-valley capacity. No source retrieved here said the Indio DAP or RUHS sites serve Mecca, Thermal, Oasis, or North Shore specifically.

## Strength 5. A valley LGBTQ community center whose Palm Springs origin was a senior gay and bisexual men's club, with a separate East Valley youth site and a Palm Springs food bank and clinic

**Statement.** The Center states that its work stretches across the valley, from the McDonald/Wright Building at 1301 North Palm Canyon Drive, Palm Springs, to The Center Coachella at 1515 Sixth Street, Coachella, the Scott Hines Behavioral Health Clinic, and the Community Food Bank at 2901 E Alejo Road, Palm Springs. Its history page dates the organization to 1998 as the Prime Timers of the Desert Foundation, a social club of mature gay and bisexual men in Palm Springs; a 2005 move to Cathedral City as the Golden Rainbow Senior Center; a 2010 SAGE affiliation; a 2012 rename as the LGBT Community Center of the Desert; a 2016 Palm Springs location and opening of the Scott Hines Mental Health Clinic, plus a partnership with Alianza for the first Eastern Coachella Valley Pride in Coachella; and a 2021 Coachella satellite in the former Coachella City Hall. Strategic initiatives are ending isolation and loneliness, connecting people to services, and enriching experience. The 2024–2025 impact report, as identified by its URL, says all activities work to reduce isolation and loneliness; that affirming mental health services aim to reduce anxiety, depression, and suicidal thoughts; and that family resources in English and Spanish help families accept LGBTQ+ loved ones. In the Scott Hines section the report prints 6,413 clinic engagements, 310 unique clients, 177 new clients, and 2,076 group therapy participants. The food-bank section prints 223 tons distributed, 15,937 households served, 64,662 pounds donated, and 35,464 people served, and says a community health worker assesses new clients for other services. The Center Coachella section prints 570 Coachella program participants, 363 youth participants, and 462 outreach event attendees, and says the site is a safe space for LGBTQ+ young people in the Eastern Coachella Valley, with after-school drop-in and Spanish-language family programs. The contact page says Palm Springs has no regular hangout drop-in hours other than the cyber center, while Coachella has regular youth drop-in hours, and that youth and family support there is in English and Spanish. The contact page also says the clinic accepts IEHP and Aetna and offers a sliding fee and grant-funded free counseling; that the Center does not provide medical care, transgender health care, HIV testing, or housing; and that it refers transgender patients to FQHCs and other providers.

**Geography.** Social-club origin: Palm Springs, then Cathedral City, then Palm Springs again. Food bank and behavioral health clinic building: Palm Springs. Youth drop-in and Spanish-language family programs named on the opened pages: Coachella, described by the organization as serving the East Valley, including Mecca, Thermal, and North Shore as communities it hopes to reach. That hope is not evidence of a site in those unincorporated places. The impact report's "more than 85,000" interactions are across all sites, not an East Valley count.

**Population and identity measurement.** Mission language is LGBTQ+ people. The 1998 origin is specifically mature gay and bisexual men, a sexual-orientation and age description, not a transgender service. Current pages add transgender and gender-expansive programs but, on the contact page, not medical gender care. Youth programs are age-specific. The food bank serves all residents; it is not an LGBT-only prevalence denominator. The 2016 YPAR finding is reported by the Center, not from an opened YPAR report: more than 600 Eastern Coachella Valley young people, wishes including LGBTQ+ friendly spaces and mental health support. The YPAR report's identity question was not retrieved.

**Sourced mechanism.** The Center's mechanism for the Palm Springs institution is historical: a club of mature gay and bisexual men created a place to gather, which became a senior center and then a broader LGBTQ center. Its mechanism for the Coachella site is the YPAR wish, as the Center recounts it, plus the aim of putting resources closer to East Valley residents. Its health mechanism is social and behavioral: reducing isolation and loneliness; connecting people to health care, food, and other resources; therapy aimed at reducing anxiety, depression, and suicidal thoughts; Spanish-language family support so parents and guardians can support young people. The impact report also attributes a health-promotion role for LGBTQ centers to a Kaiser Family Foundation study. That study was not opened, so it is not independent evidence here. The Center's own stated aims are the mechanism used.

**Limits.** Interaction and client counts are organizational outputs, not population rates, and the report does not define whether engagements are visits. No outcome evaluation with a comparison group was retrieved. The Center explicitly is not a medical home. A program name "Mis Amores" appeared only in unopened news snippets and is not used. Median ages printed on the Coachella page have no data source on that page and are not used.

## Strength 6. Civic inclusion is documented for Palm Springs and Cathedral City, not for the valley as a whole

**Statement.** Palm Springs Municipal Code Chapter 2.45 declares a city policy to protect people from discrimination, and defines discrimination to include sexual orientation and HIV status, among other characteristics. The Human Rights Commission was established in 1992, has nine council-appointed members, and is charged with education, intergroup programs, and recommendations that can include older adults and other at-risk populations. The code also provides a city domestic-partnership registry and says those local rights do not reduce rights from state registration or from marriage equality. The city states that agendas can be translated into Spanish on request. In a November 22, 2024 press release, the city said it scored 100 points on the Human Rights Campaign 2024 Municipal Equality Index, described as a rating of 506 cities on non-discrimination laws, the municipality as employer, municipal services, law enforcement, and leadership on LGBTQ+ equality. Cathedral City's communications page, dated November 21, 2024, said that city also scored 100, and said the review included a police liaison and a city executive liaison. That page states that Cathedral City hosts LGBT+ Days each March and that it was the first city in the Coachella Valley to require single-stall public restrooms to be gender-neutral, to fly a rainbow flag at City Hall, and to create a rainbow crossing.

**Geography.** Palm Springs code and commission: City of Palm Springs only. Cathedral City score and ordinances described: City of Cathedral City only. No opened HRC scorecard was retrieved for Indio, Coachella, La Quinta, Indian Wells, Palm Desert, Rancho Mirage, or Desert Hot Springs for 2024 or 2025. Those cities are not assigned a score.

**Population and identity measurement.** The code names sexual orientation and HIV status, and separately a 2017 ordinance, opened only as a search extract of a city PDF and therefore not used for new claims, was not the source of the gender-identity employment language. **Statement.** HARC states that the valley is a major retirement destination and that its senior population includes a large LGBQ share (Strength 1, with the data-quality note). ## Strength 9. Positive indicators that were actually retrieved, with their limits

**Statement.** Three opened sources support positive indicators. They are not a valley health-advantage profile.

First, *The Palm Springs Post* reported a November 4, 2026 City Council presentation of a Palm Springs profile from a Coachella Valley LGBTQ+ quality-of-life study. Among 542 Palm Springs respondents, out of 1,356 valley-wide completions, 79% had attended an LGBTQ+ event in the past year and 88% cited friends or chosen family as a source of belonging. The Post said 93% were full-time residents, 73% were 55 or older, and 17% identified as people of color. The same profile reported substantial unmet need, including 43% ever diagnosed with anxiety or depression. Belonging is the strength; the diagnoses are the limit, not a hidden asset.

Second, Davtyan and colleagues (2022) reported that 86 of 114 older adults living with HIV in the Coachella Valley (75%) agreed or strongly agreed that they saw no problem with receiving a COVID-19 vaccine if one became available, in an anonymous online survey from September 2020 to February 2021. Among those who agreed, mean age was 62.2 years (SD 7.20), 86% identified as White, 95% as male, 91% had more than a high-school education, and 31% had annual income under $20,000.

Third, DAP's harm-reduction page reports program outputs, not prevalence: in 2025, 15,580 participants served, 71,062 syringes taken in, 774 referrals, and 1,497 overdose reversals. The posted mobile schedule retrieved is Palm Springs (Alejo and North Sunrise; Arenas and El Segundo; vending machines on Arenas Road and at 1695 North Sunrise Way). The FAQ says supplies are provided at various locations throughout the Coachella Valley, but no East Valley stop list was on the opened page.

**Population and identity measurement.** The Post describes "LGBTQ+" respondents and does not quote the question. Age and race are reported; sexual orientation is not separated from gender identity in the article. The linked city PDF of the profile returned no content, so question wording was not verified. ## Bottom line

The sourced strengths are institutional and geographic, not a claim that LGBTQIA+ people in the valley are uniformly healthy. The valley has a measured LGBQ concentration and, in several named cities, a high density of cohabiting same-sex couples. Palm Springs, not the east valley, holds the extreme couple rate, the Human Rights Commission language retrieved here, the trans-led drop-in that was listed, and the aging-planning organizations. Indio and Coachella have real but narrower strengths: a DAP sexual-wellness clinic with Spanish-language capacity, limited county HIV hours, and The Center Coachella's youth and Spanish-language family programs. Health mechanisms that sources actually state are antiretroviral suppression and U=U, PrEP and PEP access without cost as a barrier, Ryan White service funding, overdose reversal and syringe access, reduced isolation through a community center, family acceptance supports, advance-care planning where chosen family is not legally obvious, and municipal rules against sexual-orientation and HIV-status discrimination in two cities. None of those mechanisms was retrieved as an East Valley outcome rate.
